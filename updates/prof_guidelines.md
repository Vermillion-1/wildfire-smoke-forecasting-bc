Project Ideas
To evaluate whether your project is good or not, please ask yourself the following three questions:

Is it important? (i.e., what impacts can your project make? )
Is it challenging? (i.e., does a naive solution work very well?)
What can I learn by doing the project? (i.e., new tools, new techniques, new domain knowledge, new methodologies).
A good project should be important, be challenging, and be able to push you to learn something that you don't know before.

Note that you need to conduct a deep analysis of the data. By deep analysis, I mean you have to think deeply about your analysis results, and report some insightful and reliable findings.

Please see the example of an externally sponsored project from last year for a reference to a project scope with potential business impact.

Step-by-step Instruction
1. Proposal (4 points)
Download the Initial Plan form Download Initial Plan formLinks to an external site.
Submit the filled form to Canvas
2. Milestone Presentation (12 points)
Communication skills are super important for data scientists. Please use this opportunity to practice your communication skills.

You can think of this presentation as a mid-term report for your project. Your presentation should consist of three parts:
Motivation & Problem Framing (3 points)

Why is it an important project?

What is the core question?

Why is it challenging? 

Progress Report (2.5 points)

What have you done so far? You need to provide evidence (e.g., screenshots of the repo and commits, an initial demo, data you've collected and explored, etc.) for your progress.

Is it on schedule? You need to show the entire project schedule (a timeline) and point to where you are.

Future work & Feasibility (2 points)
What do you plan to do next? You need to show the detailed schedule of the remaining part of the project.

How to mitigate risks? You also need to discuss whether there is any risk in completing the project on time.

Besides the three parts above, you'll also need to report the member roles and the participation (0.5 points)
The remaining depends on the quality of your presentation (4 points):
Logical slide flow
Minimal jargon
Slides are readable (e.g., not crowded)
Clear takeaway message
Confident delivery
Target 5 minutes; Exceeding 6 minutes may lead to up to a 1-point deduction; Severe exceeding (>7 minutes) will lead to a 1-point deduction each minute (until you receive 0 on these 4 points).
Imagine your manager (who knows little about the technical part of data science) is sitting in the audience, you need to explain your complex project to your manager in a simple way, and make her/him feel excited about it.

Did you convey complex information in a simple way?
Did you excite and motivate the audience?
Search "how to give a good talk" on Google or ChatGPT. You will find a lot of good advice. Use them to improve your presentation.

Submission

Submit your presentation slides (PPT) to Canvas.
3. Final Presentation (15 points)
This is showtime! Make a poster to present your data product. Please make your poster look as professional as possible. Here are a few things that you can put on the poster:

Why do you do this project?

What questions do you try to answer?

What's your methodology to get the answers?

What datasets/tools do you use?

What's your data-science pipeline like?

Why is your solution good? Why does your result make sense?

What's your data product?

What have you learned through the project?

What do you plan to do if you have more time?

Design tips:

https://www.brightcarbon.com/blog/effective-academic-posters-powerpoint/Links to an external site.
Use high-quality images that help draw attention and convey important information.
Keep it simple and uncluttered, use white space, limit text and images, and avoid distracting design elements.
Use a clear and concise title to convey the topic of your project.
Organize your content logically: Your poster should be organized in a way that is easy to follow and understand. Use headings, subheadings, and bullet points to guide the reader.
Use a hierarchy of font sizes with a font that's easy-to-read from a distance, sans-serif works well.

Grading criterion

We will mainly consider (this is not a complete list of the rubrics):

Organization and logic: e.g., is the sequence of steps taken to conduct the project clear? Are the results explained and convincing?
Poster quality: e.g., is the information clearly displayed? Is it engaging and informative for an audience not familiar with the project?
Presentation: e.g., how well is the project communicated? Is the presentation easy to follow? 
Q/A: the quality of the responses to the questions during the Q/A.
4. Code & Report (15 points)
Code

Like CMPT 732, you must use a Git repository for your project. Consider to use SFU's GitHub server is a good way to get one (instructions at that link). Group members must commit their own contributions to the repo. You are encouraged to publicize and open-source your work on GitHubLinks to an external site. or similar.

In your repository, please include a file README.txt (or README.md if you prefer) indicating how we can actually test your project as well as other notes about things we should look for. If you created some kind of web frontend, please include a URL in the README.md as well.

Report

You need to submit a report giving an overview of your project. The report should have about 2000 words with the following structure:

Project Title: Come up with an attractive project title (see this pageLinks to an external site. for some tips);
Motivation and Background: Who cares about this project? Any related work?
Problem Statement: What questions do you want to answer? Why are they challenging?
Data Science Pipeline: What's your data-science pipeline like? Describe each component in detail.
Methodology: What tools or analysis methods did you use? Why did you choose them? How did you apply them to tackling each problem?
Evaluation: Why is your solution good? Why does your result make sense?
Data Product: What's your data product? Please demonstrate how it works.
Lessons Learnt: What did you learn from this project?
Summary: A high-level summary of your project. It should be self-contained and cover all the important aspects of your project.
Grading criterion

We will mainly consider (this is not a complete list of the rubrics):

Quality of the programming code in terms of readability, organization, and documentation. Is the code well-commented and adhering to standard conventions?
Technical depth: The complexity and sophistication of the techniques used in the project. Are the methods appropriate for the problem, and do they push the boundaries of the typical course content?
Assessment of the approach and results, including testing and validation of models. Does the evaluation thoroughly and convincingly show that the solution works and is robust?
The written report's comprehensiveness, clarity, and depth of detail.
